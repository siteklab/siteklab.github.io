Much of our knowledge of the auditory brainstem comes from research in animal models. While a lot of brainstem
anatomy is conserved across mammals, species-specific features are difficult to study in humans due to technical
challenges — the structures are small, deep, and move with breathing and blood flow. We use post mortem and
ultra-high field (7T+) in vivo MRI to bridge the gap between animal models and typical human neuroimaging.

With collaborators, we published the first publicly available 3-D atlases of human auditory brainstem structures
based on post mortem MRI and histology {% cite sitek2019mapping %}, and have since used diffusion MRI tractography
to characterize structural connectivity between subcortical auditory nuclei. We are currently examining vasculature
in the human dorsal midbrain (superior and inferior colliculus) to better understand how vasculature may influence
the functional MRI signal.
