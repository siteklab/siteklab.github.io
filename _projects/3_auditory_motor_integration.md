While we speak, the brain is not only generating the motor commands for speech — it's also listening to the sounds
it's creating to make sure we're saying what we intended to say. This auditory–motor integration is well studied
when auditory feedback is experimentally altered, but we're interested in how it operates during the natural
variability of everyday speech production, and where in the auditory pathway it first emerges.

Using scalp-recorded EEG during speech production and playback, auditory cortex activity tracks the acoustic
variability of self-produced speech. More recent work combining EEG and functional MRI is now testing whether this
auditory–motor modulation originates in cortex alone or extends to earlier, subcortical stages of the auditory
pathway — funded by an NIDCD Early Career Research (R21) Award.
